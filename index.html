Breathing Neuroscience Laboratory · NCBS–TIFR

How does the brain make every breath reliable—and every breathing pattern adaptable?

We study how neural circuits generate breathing rhythms, transform latent network activity into movement, integrate sensory feedback, and coordinate breathing with brain and body states.

From local circuit dynamics to whole-organism state

Our central problem

Breathing is simultaneously automatic, resilient, and exquisitely context dependent.

The apparent simplicity of breathing conceals a distributed control system. A compact brainstem circuit must generate rhythm continuously, adapt within a single breath, respond to internal and external signals, and coordinate with behaviours ranging from swallowing and vocalisation to skilled movement, arousal, and emotion.

Research programmes

Mechanism across scales

Our projects connect ion channels, dendrites, synapses, microcircuits, behaviour, and theory rather than treating them as separate levels of explanation.

01

Emergence of inspiratory rhythm

We investigate how sparse activity in the preBötzinger Complex becomes synchronised, crosses a propagation threshold, and produces an inspiratory motor command.

  • Burstlets, bursts, and attractor dynamics
  • Network topology and heavy-tailed connectivity
  • Robustness, lability, and state transitions
02

Dendritic computation

We ask how active conductances and branch-specific input integration shape the probability that distributed synaptic activity recruits a neuron—and ultimately a breath.

  • Somato-dendritic electrophysiology
  • Two-photon holographic glutamate uncaging
  • Biophysical models of input integration
03

Inhibitory circuit architecture

We examine how inhibitory neuronal subclasses, projection logic, and feedback pathways stabilise and reshape respiratory rhythm and pattern.

  • Intersectional genetics and circuit mapping
  • Cell-type-resolved electrophysiology
  • Structure–function modelling
04

Breathing across brain and body

We study how respiratory rhythms interact with sensory feedback and other behaviours, including fine motor control in humans.

  • Multisensory respiratory feedback
  • Breathing-modulated neural activity
  • Respiration during skilled behaviour
05

Theory of flexible robustness

We use dynamical systems, statistical analysis, and biologically grounded modelling to identify the principles that make vital circuits robust without making them rigid.

  • Degeneracy and identifiability
  • Low-dimensional neural dynamics
  • Model-guided experiments

How we work

One question, many experimental languages

01

Electrophysiology

Patch clamp, dendritic recording, population activity, and in vivo neural signals.

02

Optical perturbation

Two-photon holography, glutamate uncaging, optogenetics, and fast imaging.

03

Genetic access

Transgenic and intersectional strategies to identify and manipulate circuit components.

04

Behaviour & physiology

Breathing monitoring in mice and humans, synchronised with neural or motor measurements.

05

Computation & theory

Biophysical modelling, dynamical systems, signal processing, and statistical inference.

Principal Investigator

Dr Sufyan Ashhad

Reader F (Assistant Professor), Department of Neuroscience, NCBS–TIFR

Sufyan trained in cellular neurophysiology and dendritic computation at the Indian Institute of Science, and in respiratory circuit neuroscience with Jack L. Feldman at UCLA. His laboratory integrates experiments and theory to understand how breathing circuits remain robust while rapidly switching between physiological and behavioural states.
